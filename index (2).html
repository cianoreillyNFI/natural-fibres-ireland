How to get a hemp licence in Ireland

The current route in plain English, with links to the authority that actually issues and manages the licence.

The short answer

Industrial hemp cultivation requires an annual controlled-drug licence. The Department of Health is the competent authority and the Health Products Regulatory Authority (HPRA) manages applications and inspections on its behalf.

DAFM stated on 8 July 2026 that cultivation is limited to varieties in the EU Common Catalogue with less than 0.3% THC.

Before you apply

Variety

Confirm it is in the EU Common Catalogue and meets the current Irish requirements.

Seed

Use certified seed and keep the official seed label and purchase documentation.

Site

Have the cultivation-site and land details required by the current application form ready.

End use

Be ready to explain whether the crop is intended for fibre, seed or another permitted purpose.

Security & records

HPRA licensing includes requirements around storage, security, documentation and records.

Fibre and seed only

This guide is about hemp grown for fibre and seed. Leaves and flower remain controlled material under the current framework.

DAFM stated that cultivation applicants must undertake not to process or sell those controlled parts and that they must be destroyed on cultivation. If your interest is CBD, cannabinoids, flower or leaf, contact the HPRA directly.

8 JULY 2026

Use dated sources

DAFM's statement to the Oireachtas is the clearest current public snapshot we found: hemp is eligible under BISS, permitted varieties must be in the EU Common Catalogue and have less than 0.3% THC, and approximately 11 hectares were recorded through BISS in 2026.

Some older Irish pages still refer to 0.2%. Use the latest HPRA and DAFM guidance and the conditions on your actual licence.

Common ways to make the process harder

Applying too late

The licence must be in place before cultivation. Apply well ahead of the sowing window.

Incomplete supporting information

Use the current HPRA form as your checklist.

Buying seed first and checking later

Confirm the variety before committing to seed.

No plan for controlled plant material

Leaves and flower cannot simply move into a fibre-and-seed supply chain.

Poor record keeping

Keep the licence, seed labels, invoices and land records together.
