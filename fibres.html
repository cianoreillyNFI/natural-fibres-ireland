Fibres

Five materials. Different value chains.

The point is not to put every natural fibre into every product. It is to understand where each one performs, how it needs to be processed and what a buyer will actually specify.

Bast fibre + woody core

Hemp

Growing hemp? Read the licence guide →

One stem can give long bast fibre, shorter technical fibre and shiv. That mix of outputs is useful — but only when harvesting, retting, decortication, cleaning and grading are under control.

Where it may fit
Construction · insulation · technical textiles · non-wovens · biocomposites
What needs solving
Accessible primary processing and consistent grades.
Bast fibre

Flax

Flax links Ireland's linen history with modern technical-textile and composite uses. Quality depends on how the crop is retted, scutched, hackled and prepared.

Where it may fit
Linen · technical textiles · non-wovens · insulation · biocomposites
What needs solving
A modern route from crop to repeatable fibre quality.
Animal fibre

Wool

Ireland already produces wool. The opportunity is to match grades that are undervalued in textile markets with products that need bulk, resilience, acoustic or thermal performance.

Where it may fit
Insulation · acoustic products · felts · non-wovens · composites
What needs solving
Processing routes and product specifications that create value for lower grades.
Woody / lignocellulosic fibre

Willow

Willow is not a bast fibre. Its relevance is as a fast-growing woody feedstock with routes into fibreboard, cellulose, composites and other biobased products.

Where it may fit
Panels · fibreboard · cellulose · biocomposites · biochar · biorefining
What needs solving
Finding higher-value material routes alongside bulk biomass uses.
Bast fibre

Nettle

Nettle has a long European history as a fibre plant. Modern opportunities depend on reliable cultivation or collection, retting and fibre separation that can deliver repeatable quality.

Where it may fit
Textiles · blended yarns · technical fibres · non-wovens · biocomposites
What needs solving
Modern processing knowledge and dependable feedstock.

Natural is not a performance claim

A material still has to work. Moisture, fire, strength, durability, emissions, toxicity, cost, recyclability and manufacturing compatibility all matter depending on the product.

The useful question is not “is it natural?” but “does it perform, can it be made consistently, and is there a credible route to scale?”
