The material base

Five fibres worth understanding better.

Hemp, flax, wool, willow and nettle are not interchangeable. Each has different strengths, processing needs and routes to market. The job is to understand where each one can genuinely compete.

01

Bast fibre

Hemp

Read the hemp licence guide →

A fast-growing annual crop that can yield long bast fibre, shorter technical fibre and a woody core known as shiv.

The opportunity is substantial, but commercial value depends on reliable harvesting, retting, decortication, cleaning and grading. Growing the crop is only one part of the system.

Potential applications
Construction · insulation · technical textiles · non-wovens · biocomposites · bedding
Critical gap
Accessible primary processing and consistent fibre grading.
02

Bast fibre

Flax

Flax connects Ireland's linen heritage with a new generation of technical-textile and composite applications.

As with hemp, the crop is only the starting point. Retting, scutching, hackling and consistent fibre quality determine where it can compete.

Potential applications
Linen · technical textiles · non-wovens · insulation · biocomposites
Critical gap
A modern processing route capable of producing repeatable fibre quality.
03

Animal fibre

Wool

Ireland already produces wool. The question is how more of that resource can move into useful, higher-value material applications.

Lower-value grades may have routes into insulation, acoustic products, felts, non-wovens and blended composites when processing and product requirements are aligned.

Potential applications
Thermal insulation · acoustic products · felts · non-wovens · composites
Critical gap
Matching wool grades and preparation methods to commercially viable products.
04

Woody fibre

Willow

Willow is not a bast fibre. Its opportunity lies in fast-growing lignocellulosic biomass and wood fibre.

Ireland's experience with willow as a perennial crop creates a useful base for examining higher-value material routes alongside established land-management and energy uses.

Potential applications
Fibreboard · panels · pulp and cellulose · biocomposites · biochar · biorefining
Critical gap
Identifying material routes that create more value than bulk biomass alone.
05

Bast fibre

Nettle

Nettle has a long but largely forgotten history as a fibre plant in Europe.

Its stems contain bast fibres with potential in textiles and technical materials. The challenge is developing modern cultivation, harvesting, retting and separation routes that can deliver consistent quality economically.

Potential applications
Textiles · blended yarns · technical fibres · non-wovens · biocomposites
Critical gap
Modern processing knowledge, reliable feedstock and repeatable fibre separation.

Material discipline

Natural is a starting point, not a performance claim.

The useful question is whether a material delivers better whole-life performance with a credible route to scale.

That means testing what matters for the product: moisture, fire, strength, durability, toxicity, recyclability, emissions, cost and manufacturing compatibility.

Volumes, quality, seasonality and current uses matter more than a long list of theoretical applications.
