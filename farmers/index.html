Diversification

Could fibre work on your farm?

Interest in fibre crops is growing again, but the market is not mature. Here is what is realistic today, what is still missing, and the questions to answer before you plant.

Talk to usRead the hemp licence guide

Where things stand

The interest is real. So is the work still to be done.

The Oireachtas Joint Committee on Agriculture and Food held hemp-production hearings on 1 and 8 July 2026. Evidence from growers, processors, researchers and manufacturers repeatedly returned to the same issue: Ireland needs a workable route from farm to processing and market.

DAFM told the Committee that hemp is agronomically suited to many parts of Ireland and is eligible under BISS, but the area remains small—approximately 11 hectares in 2026.

Natural Fibres Ireland exists to help close the value-chain gap, not to sell a crop.

The honest position

What we would want to know if it were our land.

There is demand, but the Irish supply chain is thin.

At the July hearings, a witness highlighted an Irish hemp-insulation example where raw material is sourced through mainland Europe rather than Irish farms. That illustrates both market interest and the domestic supply gap.

Irish acreage is currently very small.

DAFM reported approximately 11 ha of hemp in the 2026 BISS data. This is an emerging sector, not a mature commodity market.

Processing is the bottleneck.

Teagasc and industry evidence both identified the lack of local decortication and fibre-processing capacity as a major barrier to scale.

Do not plant without a route to market.

Until processing and offtake are more dependable, a contract, trial agreement or clearly identified end user is the sensible starting point.

The fibres

What each route asks of you.

Hemp

Requires an annual controlled-drug licence before cultivation under the current system. Approved low-THC varieties can produce fibre, shiv and seed, but processing access is essential.

Licence guide →

Flax

Ireland has deep linen heritage, but a modern route needs retting, scutching and fibre-quality capability aligned to end markets.

Wool

The resource already exists. The opportunity is to create higher-value routes for suitable grades in insulation, acoustics, felts, non-wovens and composites.

Rush

Where management already creates a biomass stream, rush can be examined for separate outer-fibre and inner-pith fractions. This remains early-stage product development.

The two questions

Who buys it, and what happens to my payments?

Who buys it?

Today, buyers are limited and often project-based. We can help identify current trials, processors or manufacturers when there is a genuine fit. If there is no credible buyer, we will say so.

What about BISS?

DAFM confirmed to the Oireachtas on 8 July 2026 that hemp is an eligible crop under BISS. The 2026 BISS scheme still requires the normal eligible-hectare and active-farmer conditions. Hemp also has crop-specific seed, variety and licensing requirements, so confirm the current position with DAFM before sowing.

How we help

What Natural Fibres Ireland can do for a grower.

Plain-English orientation on the current licence process and official contacts.
Introductions to processors, manufacturers and researchers where there is a genuine fit.
Early notice of trials, pilots and demonstrators looking for growers or feedstock.
Bring real farm constraints—harvesting, machinery, storage, price and timing—into sector-development work.
