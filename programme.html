From raw fibre to repeatable material

The work starts where the chain breaks: missing information, inaccessible processing, inconsistent material quality or no clear route to a customer.

Understand supply

What is actually available, where, in what volume and at what quality? That means growers and biomass streams, but also machinery, storage, test facilities and existing processors.

Close processing gaps

For fibre crops, primary processing is often the hinge. Harvesting, drying, retting, decortication, opening, cleaning and grading determine whether a crop becomes an industrial input or simply biomass.

We are interested in practical equipment routes, shared infrastructure and pilots that answer a real processing constraint.

Prove materials in use

Prototype first, then test what matters: moisture, fire, strength, durability, acoustics, thermal performance, emissions, cost or recyclability — depending on the product.

Build a route to market

A technically good material is not enough. Standards, buyer specifications, procurement, manufacturing compatibility, logistics and repeat demand decide whether a pilot survives.

Processing and markets are not side issues

DAFM's evidence on hemp said that scaling would depend on stable markets, significant capital investment in processing capacity and collaboration across the supply chain.

That is a useful test for any fibre project: who processes it, to what specification, and who buys the output?

What we will not pretend

No guaranteed market. We would rather say “not yet” than encourage a grower to produce without a realistic outlet.

No duplication for its own sake. Where universities, farmer bodies, agencies or manufacturers already have the capability, the useful role is connection.

No automatic carbon claims. Natural fibres can be part of a carbon story, but certification depends on methodology, measurement and verification.

Carbon and traceability

We are following the EU Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming framework because it may become relevant to land management, long-lived biobased products and qualifying biochar pathways.

This is an area to understand carefully, not a revenue promise.
