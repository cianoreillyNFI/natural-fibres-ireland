Programme

The work between field and product

Natural Fibres Ireland is not trying to duplicate broad bioeconomy mapping. The focus is narrower: the practical natural-fibre chain and the projects that can strengthen it.

Supply

Know what exists

Feedstock, acreage, wool grades, woody biomass, seasonal availability, storage, machinery and existing processing capacity.

Processing

Make the middle work

Primary processing is often the hinge: retting, decortication, scutching, opening, cleaning, grading and preparation. Shared or mobile infrastructure may matter as much as new products.

Proof

Test the material that will actually be sold

Not just lab curiosity. Moisture, fire, thermal performance, strength, acoustics, durability, emissions or recyclability — whatever the intended product requires.

Market

Start with a specification

Who is buying, what do they need, what quality is acceptable, what volume is useful, and what would make an Irish source competitive?

Enable

Remove friction

Licensing, standards, skills, demonstration, shared data, funding routes and introductions can all decide whether a technically good idea moves.

Open door

Ways into the programme

You have feedstock. Tell us the material, volume, location and what happens to it now.

You can process fibre. Tell us the equipment, throughput, specifications and spare capacity.

You need a material. Tell us the performance requirement rather than just asking for “something natural”.

You have an early idea. Tell us what needs to be proven next.

Three things we will not pretend

No guaranteed market

A grower needs a route to a buyer before scale.

No automatic carbon claim

Sequestration and a certified carbon unit are not the same thing.

No need to own every idea

The useful outcome may be an introduction to someone already equipped to move it.
