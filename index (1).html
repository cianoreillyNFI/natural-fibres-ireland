How to get a hemp licence in Ireland

Industrial hemp can be legally grown in Ireland, but cultivation is licensed. This guide explains the current route in plain English and links directly to the official sources.

The short answer

You need an annual controlled-drug licence before you cultivate hemp.

The Department of Health is the competent authority for controlled drugs. The Health Products Regulatory Authority (HPRA) manages the application process and inspections on its behalf.

DAFM stated to the Oireachtas on 8 July 2026 that cultivation is limited to varieties with less than 0.3% THC, registered in the EU Common Catalogue of Varieties of Agricultural Plant Species.

Email controlleddrugs@hpra.ie

Why Health?

Hemp sits inside the controlled-drugs framework.

DAFM told the Committee that cannabis and hemp both derive from Cannabis sativa and that hemp is controlled under the Misuse of Drugs framework. That is why cultivation is currently handled through the health/controlled-drugs system rather than as a normal crop licence.

Oireachtas witnesses in July 2026 argued that the annual controlled-drugs route creates uncertainty for growers and investment. Any future change would require government and legislative action.

Scope

This guide is for fibre and seed.

The stalk, fibre and seed have a different legal treatment once separated from the plant. Leaves and flower remain controlled material under the current framework.

DAFM stated that hemp cultivation applicants must undertake not to process or sell the leaves and flower and that those controlled parts must be destroyed on cultivation. If your interest is CBD, cannabinoids, flower or leaf, contact the HPRA directly.

Before you apply

Prepare the basics before sowing season.

Confirm the current form.
Use the HPRA controlled-drugs annual licence application and check for any hemp-specific instructions.
Choose an eligible variety.
Confirm that the variety is in the EU Common Catalogue and meets the current Irish/EU hemp requirements.
Use certified seed.
Keep the official seed label and purchase documentation.
Know the cultivation site.
Have the land and site details required by the current application form ready.
Know the end use.
Be able to explain whether the crop is intended for fibre, seed or another permitted purpose and how the lawful material will be handled.
Plan security, storage and records.
HPRA licensing requirements include security, storage, documentation and record-keeping obligations.

The application

Submit to the HPRA Controlled Drugs section.

HPRA's current licensing page says annual licence applications and supporting documents are submitted by email to its Controlled Drugs section.

A first-time application may involve an inspection as a normal part of review, and routine inspections may take place afterwards.

Annual controlled-drug licence fees are payable to the Department of Health. The exact fee and payment instructions should be checked on the current HPRA fee page before submission.

Common problems to avoid

Most problems are administrative, not agronomic.

Applying too late

The licence must be in place before cultivation. HPRA does not publish a guaranteed hemp-processing time on the general licence page, and a first-time inspection may be needed. Apply well ahead of the intended sowing window.

Incomplete supporting material

Use the current form as the checklist. Missing site, activity, security, storage or supporting information can slow a controlled-drug application.

Buying the wrong seed

Confirm the variety against the current EU Common Catalogue and retain the official seed label and purchase records.

No plan for controlled plant material

The leaves and flower cannot simply enter a fibre-and-seed supply chain. Build the current licence conditions on handling and destruction into the crop plan.

Poor record keeping

Keep the licence, seed labels, invoices, land details and relevant crop records together and accessible.

After approval

Keep the crop and the paperwork together.

Follow the exact conditions on the licence issued to you.
Use the approved variety and retain the certified seed documentation.
Keep records needed for HPRA, inspection and any agricultural-scheme requirements.
Manage leaves and flower in accordance with the licence and current controlled-drugs rules.
If claiming BISS, declare the land and meet the current DAFM scheme conditions.
Renew the controlled-drug annual licence for the next cultivation year.

BISS & the 0.3% figure

Two systems overlap, so use dated sources.

DAFM's 8 July 2026 statement provides the clearest current public snapshot we found: hemp is an eligible BISS crop, cultivation is limited to Common Catalogue varieties with less than 0.3% THC, and approximately 11 ha were recorded through BISS in 2026.

Some older Irish guidance pages still refer to 0.2%. For that reason, use the most recent HPRA/DAFM guidance and your licence conditions rather than relying on an older secondary summary.

Where the rules may be heading

The licensing model is under policy scrutiny.

The Joint Committee on Agriculture and Food examined hemp production in July 2026. Witnesses argued for clearer, longer-term and more agriculture-aligned licensing, while DAFM reiterated the current legal position and the need for industry-led proposals around viable markets and processing.

As of this page's review date, we found the hearings and published submissions but no hemp-specific committee report setting out final recommendations. We will update this guide when a formal outcome is published.

Frequently asked questions

Hemp licensing in plain English.

Do I need a licence to grow hemp in Ireland?

Yes. Under the current Irish framework, industrial hemp cultivation requires an annual controlled-drug licence. The Department of Health is the competent authority and the HPRA manages the application process on its behalf.

Who handles hemp licence applications in Ireland?

The Health Products Regulatory Authority (HPRA) manages controlled-drug licence applications and inspections on behalf of the Department of Health.

How long does a hemp cultivation licence last?

The current controlled-drug annual licence must be renewed each year.

Can I grow any hemp variety?

No. DAFM stated in July 2026 that permitted varieties must be in the EU Common Catalogue and have less than 0.3% THC.

Is hemp eligible under BISS?

DAFM confirmed to the Oireachtas on 8 July 2026 that hemp is an eligible crop under BISS. You still need to meet the normal scheme conditions and the crop-specific hemp requirements.

Can I sell the flower or make CBD under a fibre-and-seed cultivation licence?

No. DAFM stated that applicants must undertake not to process or sell the controlled leaves and flower and that these parts must be destroyed on cultivation. Speak to the HPRA about any activity outside fibre and seed.
