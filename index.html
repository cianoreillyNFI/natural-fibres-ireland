Ireland grew about 11 hectares of hemp last year.

Not 11 thousand. Eleven. That is the entire declared national crop — roughly fifteen football pitches. The land is not the constraint, and neither is the agronomy. What is missing sits between the field and the factory.

Area recorded through BISS in 2026, per the Department of Agriculture, Food and the Marine's opening statement to the Joint Committee on Agriculture and Food, 8 July 2026. Source ↗

Where the chain breaks

Six links. Ireland has some of them.

  1. Grow Licensed growers, certified seed, proven agronomy
  2. Ret & dry No scaled retting or drying capacity
  3. Decorticate Nearest line is outside the State
  4. Grade No published Irish grading standard for fibre
  5. Test Lab and characterisation capability exists
  6. Buy No repeat industrial offtake at volume

What would actually help

Four things we're short of. If you have one of them, that's a reason to email — and a much better one than "get involved".

Tonnages, not enthusiasm

If you grow, bale or store fibre crops: how much, what quality, where, and what you currently do with it. Even rough figures. The national picture does not exist and cannot be built from public data alone.

Idle machinery

Decortication, hammer milling, baling, drying, carding. Working or not, in use or parked in a shed. Knowing what iron is already on the island changes what is worth proposing.

A specification you can't currently fill

If you manufacture and you have a material requirement — insulation lambda value, fibre length, moisture tolerance, a price ceiling — that is the most useful message we can receive. Demand is the scarce input.

A correction

If something on this site is out of date or wrong, tell us and we will change it and say when. That applies especially to the hemp licensing guide, where the rules are moving.
