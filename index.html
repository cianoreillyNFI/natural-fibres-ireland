Our mission

Build a practical natural-fibre sector in Ireland.

Natural Fibres Ireland is a place for growers, processors, researchers, manufacturers and communities who want to turn Irish fibres into useful materials and products.

The aim is simple: connect the people, knowledge and processing needed to move fibre from field to market.

Explore the fibres Get involved →

Fibres are not new. Wool, flax, wood and plant fibres have been used for centuries in clothing, paper, rope, furniture and buildings.

What is new is the opportunity. Better processing, modern material science and demand for lower-fossil products are giving natural fibres new relevance.

Fibres

Five materials we are focusing on

They are different materials with different uses. The job is to find where each one makes sense.

Hemp

Bast fibre + shiv

Long and short fibre, plus the woody inner shiv. Uses include construction, insulation, technical fibre and composites.

Flax

Bast fibre

Best known for linen, but also relevant to technical textiles, non-wovens and composites.

Wool

Animal fibre

Already produced across Ireland. Beyond textiles, wool has potential in insulation, acoustics, felts and non-wovens.

Willow

Woody fibre

A woody feedstock with routes into fibreboard, cellulose, composites and biochar.

Nettle

Bast fibre

An old European fibre with potential in textiles and technical materials, but modern Irish processing is still at an early stage.

Carbon

Natural fibres store biogenic carbon. That does not automatically make a carbon credit.

Plants take carbon dioxide from the atmosphere as they grow. Some of that carbon can remain stored in soils, durable materials or permanent-removal pathways such as qualifying biochar.

The EU Carbon Removals and Carbon Farming (CRCF) framework is creating rules for how those climate benefits can be measured and independently certified.

Carbon farmingLand-management activities must meet an applicable methodology. Growing a fibre crop on its own is not automatically a credit.
Long-lived productsThe EU framework includes carbon stored for at least 35 years in long-lasting products. Bio-based construction materials are one area being developed.
BiocharBiochar Carbon Removal already has an EU CRCF methodology, subject to feedstock, process, monitoring and permanence rules.
EU CRCF overview ↗ Read Regulation (EU) 2024/3012 ↗
What does this mean for Ireland?

DAFM is developing a National Carbon Farming Framework. For natural fibres, the opportunity is likely to depend on the whole system: land management, traceability, processing emissions, product life and the relevant certification methodology.

Programme

Focus on the gaps that stop fibre moving

We are interested in practical problems rather than creating workstreams for the sake of it.

Supply

What fibre is available, where, in what quantity and at what quality?

Processing

Can it be retted, decorticated, opened, cleaned, graded, dried or otherwise prepared at useful scale?

Testing

Does the material meet the performance requirements of the product it is intended for?

Market

Who will buy it, in what form, and what needs to be true for them to buy it again?

A useful project starts with a real constraint. A feedstock with no outlet. A processor looking for supply. A manufacturer trying to replace a material. A prototype that needs proper testing.
